Pharmacy Sales & Inventory Analysis

Business questionWhich products drive revenue and profit, how does demand move through the year, and where is stock running short or tying up cash?

$246.1KRevenue, 61.7% gross margin
5.4×Cold & flu demand, December vs June
6Products below reorder level at year end

What I did

  1. Designed a three-table relational database (products, inventory, sales).
  2. Ran SQL data-quality checks for invalid quantities, prices and orphan records.
  3. Wrote 7 analysis queries: margins, top sellers, seasonality, days of stock cover, restock alerts.
  4. Rebuilt the summaries in Calc with SUMIFS and COUNTIFS so they update when the data changes.

Key findings

  • Demand is strongly seasonal. Allergy sales peak in May at 4.3× the January low; cold and flu peaks in December.
  • Stock was short at the worst time. The cold and flu liquid sat at 14 units against a reorder level of 30, in peak flu season.
  • $1,518 is tied up in slow movers, mostly medical devices; one item had over 2,000 days of cover.

SQL: days of stock cover 
-- Slow-moving stock: days of cover based on Oct–Dec sales
WITH recent AS (
    SELECT product_id, SUM(quantity) / 92.0 AS avg_daily_units
    FROM sales
    WHERE sale_date BETWEEN '2025-10-01' AND '2025-12-31'
    GROUP BY product_id
)
SELECT p.product_name,
       i.stock_on_hand,
       ROUND(i.stock_on_hand / NULLIF(r.avg_daily_units, 0), 0) AS days_of_cover,
       ROUND(i.stock_on_hand * p.unit_cost, 0)                  AS stock_value_at_cost
FROM inventory i
JOIN products p ON p.product_id = i.product_id
LEFT JOIN recent r ON r.product_id = i.product_id
WHERE i.stock_on_hand / NULLIF(r.avg_daily_units, 0) > 90
ORDER BY days_of_cover DESC;

Data Cleaning & Validation

Business questionThree stores merged their loyalty customer lists into one file. Can it be trusted for SMS marketing and for valuing outstanding points?

0.2% → 82%Rows passing every validation check
4,275Changes logged with before and after values
63Duplicate rows removed or merged

What I did

  1. Profiled the raw file with 11 validation checks written as Calc formulas.
  2. Standardized names, emails, phone numbers, states, ZIP codes, dates and opt-in codes.
  3. Removed exact duplicates and merged customers who had enrolled at two stores.
  4. Re-ran the same checks and sent records the data could not fix to a staff follow-up list.

Key findings

  • Only 2 of 1,215 rows met every standard. Most problems were format clashes between the three store systems.
  • Blank SMS consent was treated as “No”. Texting without recorded consent creates legal risk under US telemarketing rules (TCPA).
  • 258 records need staff follow-up, such as missing emails or negative points balances.

Customer Satisfaction Survey Analysis

Business questionWhat drives customer satisfaction, and why does the Merrillville store get fewer recommendations?

−0.13Satisfaction points per extra minute of waiting
15.7 minAverage wait at Merrillville, vs 9.9 at Highland
R² .33Multiple regression, N = 621

What I did

  1. Ran descriptive statistics and an independent-samples t-test.
  2. Compared wait times across stores with a one-way ANOVA and Tukey post hoc tests.
  3. Tested store against willingness to recommend with a chi-square test.
  4. Built a multiple regression to separate the effects of wait time, service and store.

Key findings

  • Wait time is the strongest single driver of satisfaction (r = −.42).
  • Merrillville's problem is its wait, not its location. Its gap disappears once wait time is held constant (p = .89).
  • Service adds on top. Each point of staff helpfulness adds 0.58 points; pharmacist counseling adds 0.48.

US Inflation Forecast

Business questionWhere is US consumer price inflation headed over the next 12 months, and how far can a forecast be trusted?

3.7%CPI inflation, September 2026 (actual)
4.2%Forecast for September 2027
AR(7)Model chosen after diagnostic tests

What I did

  1. Used 153 months of official CPI data and filled the October 2025 gap left by the government shutdown.
  2. Tested for stationarity with Augmented Dickey-Fuller tests.
  3. Compared AR(1)–AR(12) models and rejected AR(1) because its residuals failed the Ljung-Box test.
  4. Tested the model on two years of unseen data, then forecast 12 months ahead with 95% intervals.

Key findings

  • Inflation is likely to stay well above the Fed's 2% target, between 3.6% and 4.7% through September 2027.
  • The model beat both simple benchmarks on unseen data: 6% more accurate than assuming 2% inflation.
  • Its main limit is shocks. Trained to 2024, it expected 2.8% by September 2026; the actual figure was 3.7%.

' Compare AR(1) to AR(12) on a common sample
smpl 2015m02 2026m09
matrix(12, 3) m_criteria
for !p = 1 to 12
  equation eq_ar!p.ls infl c infl(-1 to -!p)
  m_criteria(!p, 2) = eq_ar!p.@aic
  m_criteria(!p, 3) = eq_ar!p.@schwarz
next

' Final model, diagnostics and 12-month forecast
